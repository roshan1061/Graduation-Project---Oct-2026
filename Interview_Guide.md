# Interview Guide — First-Time SIP Continuation

## Sampling
Recruit 5–6 adults in India who started their first mutual-fund SIP within the last 12–18 months. Seek variation in current status: still active, paused/reduced, failed instalment, or stopped. Prefer people who use a digital investment platform. Do not recruit solely from friends with identical backgrounds; document recruitment channel and limitations.

## Consent and privacy script
“I’m researching how people experience their first SIP, not evaluating your investment choices. Participation is voluntary; you can skip any question or stop at any time. Please do not share PAN, account numbers, bank details, login credentials, portfolio screenshots, or exact holdings. I’ll anonymise notes and use them only for this product-research assignment. Is it okay to proceed and take anonymised notes?”

Record consent, not sensitive details.

## 30–40 minute semi-structured guide
1. Tell me about the first time you decided to start a SIP. What was happening then?
2. What did you expect would happen in the first month? In six months? How did you form those expectations?
3. What made you choose a SIP rather than another way of investing? Who or what influenced you?
4. Walk me through the last time you checked the investment app. What were you trying to understand?
5. What do you usually check (if anything), and what makes you check it?
6. Was there a moment when the experience felt confusing, disappointing, worrying, or unexpectedly easy? Please walk me through that specific episode.
7. Have you ever considered pausing, reducing, or stopping an instalment? What happened just before that thought?
8. If an instalment failed or you needed cash, what did you do next? What sources or people did you consult?
9. What did you understand from the app's status, notifications, or explanations? What remained unclear?
10. What workaround did you use outside the app (search, YouTube, friends/family, bank support, spreadsheets, reminders)?
11. What helped you continue, restart, or feel confident? What did not help?
12. If the app could have helped at that moment, what would you have needed it to explain or make easier?
13. Is there anything I have misunderstood about your experience?

## Interview technique
- Ask for concrete past episodes, not only hypothetical preferences.
- Use neutral probes: “What happened next?”, “How did you interpret that?”, “What made that important?”
- Avoid leading prompts such as “Did market losses make you stop?”
- Do not ask for exact salary, account values, PAN, account numbers, or holdings. Use broad self-described categories only if relevant and voluntary.
- Separate direct quotes, paraphrase, researcher interpretation, and confidence.
- Do not imply that continuing a SIP is always the right outcome; affordability and changed goals are legitimate.
